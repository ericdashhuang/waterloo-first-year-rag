Find your co-op job
Your job search is an important step in your co-op journey. There are many ways to find a co-op position, and every student's path will look a little different. You have the flexibility to take charge of your job search and use the methods that work best for you.
As a Waterloo student, you can use WaterlooWorks, our online career platform, to find job opportunities, connect with employers, attend networking events and access career resources and support throughout your career journey. Whether you're applying through WaterlooWorks, connecting directly with employers or exploring alternative opportunities, we're here to support you every step of the way.
Job search expectations
Finding a co-op job takes time, effort and persistence. Consider dedicating the same amount of time to recruiting as you would for an academic course. Setting aside regular time each week for your job search can help you stay organized and manage timelines.
Every hiring process is a little different, but most opportunities involve submitting an application, participating in one or more interviews and waiting for the employer to make a decision. Many students apply for jobs while also interviewing for others at the same time. Not every application leads to a job, but each one helps you build skills and prepare for future opportunities.
Before you start, review important dates and work term requirements so you can stay on track for co-op credit. If you have questions at any stage of the process, your Co-op Advisor is here to help.

Prepare for your job search
Before you begin your job search, take time to reflect on your goals, interests and priorities. Consider factors that may influence your search, such as location, housing, cost of living, salary expectations and preferred industries.

Consider working for the Government of Canada
The Government of Canada hires students across a wide range of fields, including business, technology, policy, science, communications and more. These opportunities can provide meaningful work experience, professional development and the chance to contribute to projects that impact Canadians.
Explore available programs and learn how to apply for student and co-op opportunities with federal departments and agencies.
AI use in the recruitment process
Some employers may use artificial intelligence (AI) tools to help screen or review application materials. CEE asks employers to protect student privacy and confidentiality in accordance with University Policy 46 .
